# SlashySlash 3D

A third-person 3D version of [SlashySlash](https://github.com/IanSzymanski/slashyslash): a Risk of Rain 2 style orbit camera and a Half-Life 1 style low-poly look, built with Three.js. The 2D game stays in its own repo and is the main game; this repo is the 3D lane. The build plan lives in the 2D repo at [`docs/3d-plan.md`](https://github.com/IanSzymanski/slashyslash/blob/main/docs/3d-plan.md).

**Status: all six phases of the plan are built; the rival is a first pass, open to tuning.** Every mode starts in the crossroads, a 70 x 70 m arena built for the orbit camera, with baked light that flickers, rain and ash, a Blackout twist and a Retro look. The 2D street, converted to 3D in Phase 1, is still there as a free roam. You have the player's full move set: strings that follow the aim up and down, slide attacks, plunge, dash slash, parry and counter, the tether (fling, zip, barrel throws, grenade kicks, the crane), bullet jump, skid turns, side vaults, ledge climbs, wall cling and wall kick, and the charged sword beam. Street lamps, barrels, bins and site gear collide, and lamps can be cut down. All eight 2D enemy types are in, each with its tells and its answer, and they follow you across the roofs, climbing fire escapes and dropping off ledges. The Wave Demo and Endless Survival play as in 2D, with endless upgrades and wave twists. The player is the armoured swordsman from the concept sheets, with poses ported from the 2D game and a cloth scarf. The rival AEGIS-07 is in: a 2.5 m mech from its reference sheets that boosts on back thrusters, keeps its distance in phase 1 and presses in in phase 2, and chains sideways dashes into its cuts, with both of its 2D phases, its cutscenes, the Rival Fight and the Melee Duel. The scroll wheel zooms the camera, all the way in to first person, where the screen itself reacts to your swings, dashes and hits. Training opens with a guided tutorial through a bright three-room training building (a movement course that winds up round its first room, a tether room with a football machine, and a combat hall) and then becomes a practice area; the Sawblade ultimate is in; and the game plays on phones and tablets with touch controls. AEGIS-07 is also playable, picked on the title screen: a mech that switches between a gunner mode (machine gun and a shield-draining beam) and a melee mode (a wrist blade, a block and a pulse burst), with jet dashes, a hover and an orbital strike ultimate. The second boss, KRAG, the Molten Fist, is in as a first pass: a 2.9 m stone-and-magma brawler from its concept sheets with an onyx left arm, heat that ends in the Molten Fist and an onyx arm that cracks as you hit its guard (both shown only on its body), a guard you bait and blow up in its face, shoulder charges, stomps that split the street in glowing cracks and raise molten vents, and from far off leaps and a chain it swings round posts on, in its own KRAG Fight. It has its own region, the Foundry: a forge hall split by a river of molten iron, with gantry cranes whose ladles you can tip, conveyors, crucibles of slag and a blast furnace. Endless travels there through a portal when AEGIS-07 falls, and KRAG comes three waves later; a portal back opens when KRAG falls.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser: on a desktop with a keyboard and mouse, or on a phone or tablet with touch (landscape). No build step or server is needed; Three.js is vendored as a classic script so the page runs from `file://`. WebGL 2 is required.

If your browser blocks local files, serve the folder instead:

```sh
npx serve .
```

**The crossroads.** Four streets 18 m wide meet at a plaza raised a kerb's height. A block of four buildings fills three of the corners, split by 6 m alleys, with walkable roofs from 7 to 18 m. The south-east corner is a construction site behind plywood hoarding (three gates): a three-storey steel frame, scaffolding that carries the overpass on into the site, an excavator, a dirt pile, stacks of beams, pipes, bricks and timber, a mixer, site offices, port-a-potties and two floodlight towers. A tower crane stands by the plaza corner with its jib 25 m up over the east street and a bundle of beams hanging from its hook at 10 m; the tether reaches it from twice as far as anything else. An overpass crosses the east street at 7 m, girders cross the other three, and fire escapes climb every building taller than an enemy's jump, so every roof is a route for you and for them. Container barricades close the street ends against a ring of facades too tall to climb. Lamps light the plaza and streets, neon signs face the plaza, and barrel fires burn in the alleys.

**The Foundry.** The second region, KRAG's: a forge hall about 72 x 64 m with iron walls 30 m tall under an open roof of trusses, its layout nothing like the crossroads'. A river of molten iron, 6 m wide and sunk 1.6 m below the floor, flows its whole length from the blast furnace's tap on the west wall (molten metal pouring down from the tap into the river's head) to a drain glowing under the east wall, crossed by three grated bridges. Anything that goes in is burned and thrown back out onto the nearer bank: you take a little, an enemy you knock in takes a lot (and it's worth style), a crucible bursts, KRAG just climbs out. Enemies won't walk into it; they take the bridges. North of the river: two yellow gantry cranes on rails 14 m up, each with a ladle of molten metal hanging from its hook, an ore hopper, stacks of ingots, a scrap heap and a rolling mill with a great flywheel, huge gears turning on the wall above. Hit a ladle (a jumping slash reaches it, as does a blast under it) or yank it on the tether and it tips toward the side the blow came from and pours: the stream burns whatever is under it, hard, and spreads into a pool of molten slag that burns whatever stands in it; it hangs empty until the next wave fills it. The gantries have tether points under them to zip or be flung up onto. South of the river: two conveyors running opposite ways that carry whatever stands on them (you, enemies, crucibles), rows of casting molds with metal cooling in them, a hydraulic press whose crosshead you can climb onto, and a storage rack stepping up to the catwalk. Catwalks run 7 m up along the north and south walls, and the furnace has its own up its face to its top at 20 m. Instead of red barrels, crucibles of slag stand about the floor: they light, throw and burst like barrels, but burst into a pool of slag. Instead of rain, embers rise through the hall; instead of street lamps, sodium work lamps hang high (a Blackout puts them out; the molten metal still glows).

**Modes.** *Wave Demo* is the 2D demo's six waves, each built around one problem a move solves (shields, low armour, grenades, snipers on high ground), and a seventh that brings the rival. *Endless Survival* grows waves from a budget, adds a type every wave or two (all eight by wave 6) and a brute wave every fifth. After waves 2, 5, 8 and so on you pick one of three upgrades, and from wave 4 a wave can carry a twist: Elites (gilded, tougher, worth double), Glints Only (the threat ring shows glints and nothing else), Low Gravity, or Blackout (the street lamps go out for the wave; fires and neon still burn, and you carry a faint light). Endless travels between the two regions. It starts in the crossroads, where wave 8 is AEGIS-07; when it falls a portal opens on the plaza (an arrow at the screen's edge points to it), and stepping through takes the run to the Foundry, score, wave count, upgrades, health, shield and the ultimate's charge carried over. Three waves there, then KRAG; when KRAG falls a portal opens back to the crossroads, where after three waves AEGIS-07 comes again, and so on round, the bosses tougher each time (a toggle in Settings turns the bosses off; the portal still opens after each region's last wave). *Rival Fight* is AEGIS-07 alone; *Melee Duel* skips its shielded first phase and fights its blade phase at full health; *KRAG Fight* is KRAG alone, in the Foundry. *Free Roam* is the city with no enemies, with a portal on the plaza to the Foundry and one there back (step through to go); *The 2D Street* is a free roam on the converted 2D level. Each city arena has its red barrels; the Foundry has crucibles.

**Training.** The first time you open it, Training runs a guided tutorial through a bright training building of three rooms, each move with a station built for it. Each step puts one short line of text at the top of the screen and, where there's a place to get to, a marker in the world (an arrow at the screen's edge points to it when it's out of view), then waits for you to do it.

*The movement hall* (36 m tall) winds up round its own walls. 1. move to a marker; 2. climb a 2.5 m block by walking into it; 3. jump onto a 5.5 m block; 4. double jump from there to a 13 m tower in the corner. From the tower a walkway runs down one wall and along the next: 5. slide through the gap under a wall (too low to walk or crouch through); 6. vault through a slot above a 1 m barrier (solid below, so no slide gets under it; too low to jump through standing; and it can't be climbed onto); 7. dash through a doorway of red lasers (walking into them throws you back); 8. wall kick up the pocket at the walkway's end, from the purple wall to the purple pier and back, onto a deck 11.5 m higher than a double jump reaches; 9. run off the deck's end and cross the 42 m gap through the opening into the next room: a double jump with one dash, at any timing, falls short, and a double jump with two dashes after its peak clears it.

*The tether room*: the tether comes free here (the tutorial holds it back until now, and the bullet jump until the combat hall, so neither can skip the course). 10. zip up a 15 m ledge onto a football field with a held tether; 11. a battered ball machine, taped together on a tripod with one bent leg, lobs footballs at you, each throw shown first as a dashed arc with the spot it lands on marked. Tether a ball in the air and time slows while you reel in and kick it where the crosshair points; put two between the goal posts and over the bar and the shutter under the posts opens.

*The combat hall*: drop down to the dummy. 12. land a three-hit string on it; 13. a heavy; 14. take down two scrappers (they appear in front of you, and the dummy steps off its mat until they're down); 15. hit the dummy with a fully charged sword beam; 16. parry its yellow flash twice; 17. parry and then counter; 18. get past its red flash twice by dashing through its body to its back (a parry does nothing against red, and a dash's protection doesn't save you in front of it).

No step can be skipped by failing it or going round it. Every step has a checkpoint and the ground it allows: fall off the walkway, come up short on the gap, drop off the field, or land past a station without going through it, and you're put back at the step's checkpoint to try again (the step's title flashes red, and on the gap a second line says when to dash). Kicks and goals you've already scored are kept. The walls of the first two rooms give no grip outside the wall-kick station, so a kick can't save a short jump.

The steps use your own key bindings, or the touch buttons' names on a touch screen. The tutorial teaches RONIN's moves, so it plays as RONIN whichever character is picked; skipping or finishing it hands the hall back to the picked one. When the last one is done, the combat hall becomes a practice area and later visits open straight into it (the door stays open, so the course is there to replay on your own). The dummy stands on a mat in front of a dark wall, so its flashes read: it can stand still (with a damage-per-second readout over it) or swing at you with yellow flashes, red flashes, both, or fast yellow ones. Pause opens the practice panel: spawn any enemy, AEGIS-07 (either phase) or KRAG (cold, or already at full heat) in front of you, set the dummy, cycle a wave twist, refill, charge the ultimate, clear the hall, toggle upgrades, and replay (or, mid-tutorial, skip) the tutorial. You can't die in Training: no hit takes your last point of health, and health comes back after a few seconds unhurt.

**Sawblade.** Kills fill the ultimate meter under your health bars (brutes count triple, and your style rank multiplies it). When it's full, the teeth spin and flash; hold `Q` for a second (lightning closes in on you) to become the 2D game's red sawblade for 7 seconds. The camera pulls back to third person and the saw faces it, spinning the way it rolls. You can't be hurt; a burst on the way in throws back everything within 8.6 m. You roll at up to 27 m/s, jump as often as you like (`C` drives you down), and bounce off walls you hit hard, grinding sparks and scorch marks. Anything the disc touches is ground down, a hit every 0.09 s; attack lunges you at the crosshair. The tether costs nothing and reaches anything, and held on an enemy it latches the saw onto it and grinds, harder every tenth of a second (AEGIS-07 throws you off after a moment). A ring of dots round the crosshair counts the time down.

**Touch screens.** On a phone or tablet the game switches to touch controls by itself (Settings > Controls: Touch controls Auto, On or Off), the usual layout for a mobile action game:
- the left thumb moves: a stick appears wherever it lands, and pushing further runs (the base follows the thumb, so it never runs out of travel);
- the right thumb looks: drag anywhere on the right, or drag from a held button, so you can aim while holding attack or the tether;
- a cluster of buttons under the right thumb: ATTACK (largest), JUMP, DASH, PARRY, TETHER, BEAM (its fill shows the cooldown and the charge), SLIDE, and ULT once the meter is full. Holds work as they do on a keyboard: hold ATTACK for a heavy, TETHER to zip, BEAM to charge, PARRY for the stance;
- pause and a first/third person toggle top right; in a cutscene only PARRY stays, to hold for a skip.
- as AEGIS-07, PARRY reads BEAM in gunner mode and BLOCK in melee, BEAM reads MODE (hold it to switch, its fill showing the switch), and ULT stays up while the strike is aimed, for the press that fires it. The gun reloads itself when the magazine runs dry.

No mouse lock is needed. Starting a mode asks for full screen and landscape where the browser allows it, and turning the device upright pauses with a prompt to turn it back. The tether's aim assist is twice as wide, menus and their buttons are larger, the render resolution is capped lower on very dense screens, and hits and parries buzz where the device can. Button size and opacity, look speed, vibration and full screen are in Settings > Controls. How to Play and the tutorial name the buttons instead of keys.

**The mouse.** Starting a mode locks the mouse; moving it turns the camera. `Esc` releases it and pauses (browsers keep `Esc` to themselves while the mouse is locked, so losing the lock is what pauses). Browsers refuse to re-lock for about a second after `Esc`; if Resume doesn't take, click it again. Clicking the scene beside the pause menu also resumes. The game checks the lock every frame rather than trusting the browser to report losing it, so however it goes (`Esc`, a browser or system hotkey, switching windows), the run pauses within a third of a second and Resume takes it back; picking an endless upgrade takes it back straight away. Where the browser offers it (Chrome on Windows and macOS), the lock uses raw mouse input, so your OS pointer speed and acceleration don't apply; tune Sensitivity in Settings instead.

### Playing as AEGIS-07

The button at the top of the title screen picks who you play as (RONIN or AEGIS-07, remembered between sessions). AEGIS-07 is the rival's mech on the same controller, so everything under Movement still works (run, jump, crouch, slide, bullet jump, skid, vault, climb, wall cling, the tether, barrels and grenade kicks), on a taller body (2.4 m) with the camera a little further back. Its dashes are jet boosts, and holding jump in the air lights its jets and holds it near the top of the jump, for up to 2.6 s per airtime (letting go drops out, so a tapped double jump doesn't hover). Its light is cyan, apart from the rival's blue and RONIN's red, and its tether, zip afterimages and zip strike are cyan too. It has two weapon modes; the HUD shows which, with the beam or block on `E`, ammo at the bottom right and the strike's charge at the bottom left.

**Movement** is tuned for a third-person action feel rather than the 2D game's measured pace. You get to a run (9.5 m/s) in about a tenth of a second, and keeping it up builds to a sprint (13.8 m/s) over half a second. At a sprint the body pitches well forward, the free arm pumps and the blade trails behind. Turns carve round at speed instead of cutting across, banking the body into them. A hard reversal at speed is a skid turn: the body spins to face the new way and drops low, leaning hard onto the free hand dug into the street; sparks stream off the hand and it and the trailing boot carve marks that glow, cool and fade; when the slide is spent you push off the hand at a run. Running into a car or anything else up to chest height vaults it in stride: a hop that clears the top, the free hand planted on it and the body rolled onto its side with the knees swung over. Anything higher, or anything you walk into too slowly to vault, you climb once you've pushed into it for a tenth of a second, up to 2.65 m (a shipping container): hands on the lip, the body hauled up past them and a foot up onto the top, a quarter to a third of a second depending on the height. In the air, moving into a wall whose top is within 2.4 m of your feet grabs the edge and climbs it the same way, at any point in the jump. Jumping into a wall too tall for that clings to it: the left hand digs in above the shoulder and holds for a second, then the slide starts slowly and builds, the hand scoring a glowing groove down the wall. Wall kicks come from a cling. Jumps reach the same heights as in 2D but hang a moment at the top and carry your speed, and a hard landing at a run rolls through. The view widens a little at a sprint and trails slightly behind (the comfort toggle in Settings turns the widening off).

**First person** (the scroll wheel all the way in) looks out from the model's eyes, wherever the pose puts the head; anything of the body within 0.28 m of the lens dithers away, so you see your arms, legs and blade below you without looking out through your own shoulder. It runs 5 degrees wider than third person, its speed kicks are 40% bigger, and the screen reacts: swings punch the view wider and tip it with the swing, heavies and the finisher harder; a dash punches it wide and leans into the dash's direction, with speed lines streaking in from the edges (also on drawn slashes, zips, sprints and long falls); each hit you land gives a small punch; the screen's edges flare gold on a parry or the counter and white on a dodge behind the rival's red flash (never red for your own attacks: red at the edges only ever means you were hurt); a hit through the shield narrows the view, knocks the head away from it and darkens the edges, darkest on the side it came from, while one the shield eats glints blue; below 40 health the edges pulse dark red like a heartbeat. The tether leaves from your left hand, reaching out at the lower left of the view, and your own afterimages (dashes, zips) aren't drawn over the lens. The view's punches and roll follow the comfort toggle; the edge flares follow the flashes setting.

**Sword beam (`F`).** A tap throws a small crescent of light toward the crosshair at 44 m/s; it does 12 damage and breaks on the first enemy, bolt or strike probe it meets. Hold `F` and the blade gathers light: sparks stream onto it, its glow builds, a ring pulses tighter round your feet, a hum climbs, and a ring round the crosshair and the `F` diamond fill. Letting go early throws a bigger blade (up to 22 damage). At full charge (1.1 s) the blade flashes gold with a chime and shakes; letting go then throws a massive wave at the crosshair, up onto a roof or down off a ledge. Aimed at the ground, it comes down there and runs on along it; once past what it was aimed at, a wave heading down, level or only a little up (under about 9 degrees) settles onto the ground below it, while a steeper one keeps climbing. On the ground it follows steps up and drops down. It starts 6 m wide, widens to 19 m over its 45 m run, hits every enemy in its path once for 55 (cutting through a shield scrapper's shield and cracking a crawler's armour), burns bolts and the rival's strike probes out of the air, cuts lamp posts and cracks one of the rival's phase 1 shield plates. Only its middle meets the world: a wall or pillar in the middle of its path stops it, while its edges pass through walls and still hit what's behind them. Charging, you move at half speed and face the aim. Any hit that lands while charging (shield or health) shatters the charge, and `F` then waits 2 s; a dash, a slash, the parry stance, the tether or the ultimate drop the charge at no cost. In phase 2 the rival's raised guard blocks a beam without a counter. In first person the view narrows a little as the charge builds and the edges glow pale gold at full.

**The counter (`E`).** After a parry, keep holding `E` to charge the counter; let go to throw it. Its damage climbs with the hold: a tap hits like an opening heavy (30), and a full charge after 0.9 s hits for 135 (176 on a stunned rival). A gold ring round the crosshair fills as it charges, with a tick at each third; at each third a chime rings a step higher and light bursts off the blade, gold motes stream into the blade faster and from further away, a ring closes in round your feet, the stance tightens and shakes, and the hum climbs. At full the blade flashes white with a bell and a swell, the ring flickers and pulses, and an aura lifts off you; the counter throws itself half a second later if you keep holding. The release grows with the charge: the cut's line, the blast round you, the bursts along the cut, the shake, the slow motion and the boom are small for a tap, and a full one adds a second, white shockwave and a thunderclap. The parry's own window (a third of a second, where another attack is parried too) protects you; after that a hit shatters the charge in a spray of gold and you get no counter. Kills from the counter heal you a little. Arc Conductor halves the charge time.

Swings turn onto the nearest enemy within 30 degrees of the crosshair and close the gap to it (melee aim assist), above or below you as well as level. Without a target a swing tips up or down with the crosshair (a little dead zone keeps ordinary play level); the body pitches with it in the air and bends at the waist on the ground, using the same swing animations, so you can cut a drone overhead or something below a ledge.

**Props.** Street lamps, fire barrels, bins, hydrants, cones, red barrels and the site's gear all collide; round things are cylinders you slide round rather than stop dead on, and running into a red barrel shoves it. A slash through a lamp post, or a blast beside one, cuts it in two: its light goes out (the light bake is redone around it over a few frames), the pole stops colliding, and the top topples away from the blade or the blast, smashes into pieces on whatever it lands on and hits anything under it. A short stump stays. Lamps stand again on a new run. The tether snaps to an enemy, barrel or grenade within 4 degrees of the crosshair; the crosshair turns red when it would catch something you can pay for. Swings and dash slashes send gunner bolts back toward the crosshair, and a slash lights a barrel's fuse. Not built yet: the Sawblade ultimate (Phase 6).

### Enemies

Every melee attack glints about 0.28 s before it lands, wherever the enemy is, and `E` on the glint parries it. There is no instructional text in play (no labels for stances, shields or what to do); each enemy's state shows on its model, its effects and its sounds. Damage and heal numbers stay, and can be turned off in Settings.

| Enemy | What it does | The answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scrapper | Rushes you with a cleaver | Parry the glint, or hit first |
| Gunner | Holds 9 to 17 m away and lays a laser on you: dashed while it tracks, white once locked | Slash or parry the bolt back into it |

### AEGIS-07

The rival from the 2D game, rebuilt for open space. It is a 2.5 m mech in light and mid grey pixel camo with blue patches, from its reference sheets (toned down from their white so it sits between the street and your dark armour): a T-shaped visor and a square chest core, big pauldrons, a beam cannon on the left shoulder, a thruster pack with two round jets, and a long housing on the right forearm that is both its gun barrel and the emitter for its energy blade. It runs heavily, boosts on its thrusters, hovers, tether-zips like you do, and leaps up to whatever roof you are on.

**Stance.** Its stance shows in how it moves and in its jets, which burn brighter while it charges. In phase 1 it always zones: it holds a band of range (9 to 16 m) and strafes round you, and moves round the arena on its tether, throwing a line from its left hand to a vantage 9 to 15 m from you with a clear shot at you (the overpass, a roof edge, a container, the far side of the street) and reeling itself there. Get in its face and it cuts at you and boosts back out; keep crowding it and it zips well away. In phase 2 it always presses in, boosting along open street from far off; it only calls orbital strikes or leaps on you with the dive bomb when you try to get away (running off from it, or staying out at range). Your dashes and drawn cuts pass straight through its body: it holds its ground while you go by instead of sliding along with you.

**Dash chains.** Its thruster dashes come in chains of legs, each taken relative to where you are when it starts, so it rarely comes straight at you. It zigzags in (a boost off to one side of the line to you, then the other side, then the cut), swings round your flank and comes from the side, feints in and back out before it comes, or rushes straight. From more than 12 m off it takes long chains instead (four or five legs, longer ones while it's far, with a boost straight in if the last leg leaves it out of reach of the cut), so it crosses the street in boosts rather than leaping. Only a leg that attacks glints (the cut, or a blow thrown at the end of a chain), so it can be parried or dashed through; repositioning boosts never glint. The cut passes through where you stood.

**Phase 1 (above half health).** A shield covers it. It zones with a forearm machine gun: its barrels spin up (a ring of blades turning faster at the muzzle, a whir that climbs) while a fan of light opens over the area it will spray, then twelve bolts in a pattern round you: a sweep across you from one side to the other, a line walked in from the street in front of you and up onto you, or a loose scatter and a shoulder beam it fires hovering, which sweeps after you slowly and cuts down any street lamp in its path, so break line of sight or outrun the sweep. It lobs a pulse grenade with its arc and blast zone shown. Up close it slashes or cleaves to drive you back, then boosts out of reach. Slash its bolts back into the shield (eight cracks break it) or tether its grenade and kick it back (that breaks it at once). Broken, it's stunned for 3 s and the shield stays down for 10 s. Damage stops at half health until the enrage: a shockwave throws you clear, it rises on its jets, its three limiters blow and it slams down. It stays blue throughout; in phase 2 its guns go dark and the wiring under the limiters glows.

**Phase 2.** Shield and guns offline, blade only, with blue lightning on its swings. It fights in combos (slash, cleave with a ground ring, rising cut, thrust, spin), dash chains, orbital strikes when you run (it brings its shoulder cannon round, the lens lighting up again, and lobs two or three probes onto the street where your run is taking you and further along it; each lands, pulses under a siren for 1.25 s inside a 3.4 m ring, then erupts in a column of light that hits whoever is still inside, a controlled version of the beacon it arrives in), and dive bombs: it charges for about a second (energy drawn in round it, the street shaking, a mark already under you), leaps 16 m over you on its jets, hangs while the mark tracks you, flashes as the mark locks, and 0.3 s later boosts straight down onto it at 90 m/s, cracking the street and throwing a ring out.

**The red flash** is the one thing about it that turns red. As it winds up, a red shell closes in round its body, red rings close in on its feet, red energy streams into its blade, the light round it builds and the ground shakes, under a sound unlike anything else in the fight (a sub drop, a tritone alarm that pulses faster, a growl climbing under it, then a metal shriek and a bass hit as its eye flashes). It speeds up less than its other moves on the harder settings: its wind-up shortens by 55% of what the telegraph setting takes off the rest (8% on Hard, 17% on Nightmare).

### KRAG

The second boss, KRAG, the Molten Fist: a hulking cyborg about 2.9 m tall, from its concept sheets. It's built of rust-brown pixel stone blocks with molten seams glowing between them and cracks of lava running across them, a small helmet sunk between the shoulders with three glowing slits in its dark face plate, a hexagonal core burning in its chest, a spike off the right knee and glowing toes and soles. Its left arm is the standout: onyx plating, a pauldron twice the size of its right with a great spike on top, a ridge of spikes down the forearm, and a fist as wide as its chest, spiked across the knuckles with its plates glowing between them. A mohawk of flame rises off its crest. It's violent, rough and brutal, and unrelenting: it never backs off, its recoveries shorten as its health drops, and everywhere it walks it leaves smouldering footprints that cool from orange to nothing. It has 1,500 health (the rival has 900) and fights recklessly: it doesn't dodge, and nothing you hit it with interrupts what it's doing.

**Heat.** It runs hot, and it shows only on its body (there's no bar for it): the seams run from dull red through orange to white, steam pours off its back and its arm, embers drift off it, and the mohawk grows. Heat builds steadily from fighting on its own (about 2 a second), a little with every blow it throws, and most from blows it lands on you (10) or that you parry (6) and from its stomps; blows on its guard add none. At full heat the mohawk tears up off its head with an orange pulse and a roar, and an orange pulse comes off it every second or so. Only then can it throw the Molten Fist, and throwing it, or having the arm go off, takes it back to cold, with no heat for a few seconds after.

**The arm.** The onyx arm's durability is its own thing, also shown only on its body. Blows on its guard wear it, and you read how worn it is from the arm and from how a blow on it sounds. Whole, the plates are glossy dark onyx and a blocked blow rings off in a solid clang and sparks. As it wears, the plates go ashen and chalky, fractures split open across them glowing dull red, it smokes, and a blocked blow sounds duller, cracks under the clang and knocks chips off. Near the end the fractures burn orange to white and flicker, light crackles across it, chips drop off it as it moves, the stone creaks on its own, and a blocked blow splinters with a dry crunch and a snap. Then it breaks: the guard shatters in onyx shards for 4% of its health, it reels, and it can't guard for 8 s while the broken arm hangs dark and smoking, before molten seams run through it and it re-forms, half worn. Left alone for 4 s, a worn arm slowly mends. Breaking it means no guard to bait into a detonation until it re-forms; the blast of a detonation cracks the arm too.

**Strings.** Up close it fights in fast strings of jabs, crosses, hooks, elbows, knees and kicks, starting a string from a stride or two off with a hard step in. It stays on you through a string: it turns with you, walks in on where you're going, and if you get out of reach it steps in after you before the next blow (twice a blow at most), then drives into each blow after you. A parry doesn't stop a string blow: it drives straight through, you take no damage but you're shoved back and lose a little shield, it isn't staggered and you get no counter. Dodge them. A string blow's only tell is a flick of embers off the limb with a grunt: no gold. Each string ends on a stopper (a haymaker, an uppercut, a hammer fist with a shockwave, a spinning backfist, a flying knee), and a stopper is gold from the start of its longer wind-up: its outline turns gold, gold fire swells round the limb, gold rings close in on its feet, a deep gong swells under it, and then a big gold glint and a bell. Parry that and it's knocked back off balance for a moment, and the parry charges your counter.

**The guard.** It only blocks when it feels you're a threat: from range (a sword beam charging, AEGIS's gun firing or its beam, ranged hits landing) or right after you've staggered it. Blue means a block: a blue flash comes off the onyx arm with a cold ring and its outline flickers blue for 0.3 s while the arm snaps up, and blows still land in full until it's up. Then the onyx arm is up before its face and it walks in behind it, turning slowly, so you can get round it; crowd it in front and it shoves you off. A blow on the guard from the front does 15% of its damage and wears the arm. If it reaches full heat behind its guard, it drops the guard straight into the Molten Fist. It knows a charged arm is a liability, so at full heat it won't guard against anything less than a sword beam near full charge or AEGIS's beam (and not always against those), but a stagger still puts the arm up on instinct: the arm glows white, crackles and shakes, and its outline flickers white and orange. Hit the arm then, with anything, and all its heat goes off in its face: 12% of its health, a blast that throws it onto its back, and a 3.4 s stun sitting dazed in the street, the arm smoking, taking a third more damage. That's the only stun it has. A full counter is the classic bait: parry the stopper, hold the counter through its recoil, and it lands on the guard it raises.

**The Molten Fist** is its red flash, and red means what it means on the rival: no parry. It draws the onyx arm far back in bullet time inside a red shell, red rings closing on its feet and red energy streaming into the fist, under the rival's red flash sound and a furnace roar, and the street splits red under it as grit lifts off the ground round it. The street it will split open is one massive hard-edged red cone from its feet, about 100 degrees wide and 26 m long. It turns with you for the first half of the wind-up; then its visor flashes red with a heavy clunk and the cone holds its line, its edge flickering; just before the blow the visor flashes white. It drives the fist down into the street and the whole cone splits open into a molten chasm, the split racing out to its end in a fraction of a second, throwing fire and stone up across its whole width and tearing down the lamps in it. Anyone in the cone on its street is hit as the split reaches them, jumping or not: no parry, no dash, and it's far too long to outrun. Get out of it sideways after it locks (easiest close in, where it's narrow) or get behind it (a slow-motion white afterimage says you made it). It hits for 55 straight to health, throws you a long way and leaves you dazed for 1.5 s (sparks circle your head and nothing you press does anything). The chasm then glows molten for a few seconds, its floor erupting in vents (never right under you while you're dazed), and cools and closes. A miss leaves it hunched over the smoking arm for 1.9 s.

**Getting to you.** From further off it closes with big moves rather than small dashes, and every landing is an explosion. Up close and at mid range: a shoulder charge (the spiked pauldron dropped, a gold glint, then up to 22 m at you, tearing a furrow in the street, smashing lamps on the way and exploding when it meets you or a wall) and stomps: a foot hauled up and driven down, the street splitting in glowing cracks round the foot and out along the street at you, a shockwave running out round it and molten vents raised along the cracks, three stomps in a row more often as it rages. The cracks glow white hot, cool through orange to a dull red and fade over several seconds; every slam, landing and vent splits them too. A vent is a glowing crack that bubbles and pulses faster for half a second, then bursts into a column of fire that throws whoever stands on it into the air. Only from far off (16 m and more) does it leap or swing on its chain. The leap comes down on where you're heading (its landing ring and cross marked on the street, blinking faster as it falls, with a gold glint just before) with a blast, a shockwave and a ring of vents. Its tether isn't for getting places: a chain from its right hand bites near the top of a lamp post or onto the corner of something solid, it's hauled out onto a wide circle (8 m across where there's room, never under 5.6 m), swings round it building speed until it's swung up off its feet leaning out hard on the taut chain, its feet dragging sparks until they leave the street, then lets go and flings itself flat at you like a comet, glinting gold before it hits and landing with a blast, a shockwave and vents. A lamp post it swung on snaps as it lets go.

### Feel test (KRAG)

Play the KRAG Fight a few times, as each character. It's a first pass; notes on any of these help:

1. It feels heavy, brutal and relentless: it's always in your face, and the footprints, the stomps and every landing sell its weight.
2. The heat reads with no label: you can tell roughly how hot it is from the seams, the steam and the mohawk, and full heat is unmistakable.
3. Its strings are worth dodging rather than parrying, and gold means parry: a stopper stands out from the blows before it from the start of its wind-up, in time to parry. It stays on you through a string without feeling like it teleports.
4. Baiting the charged guard is learnable and worth it: parrying a stopper at full heat, or charging a full sword beam at range, gets the arm up, and the blast in its face is the fight's big payoff.
5. You can tell how hot it is and how worn its arm is without a bar: heat from the seams, steam and mohawk; the arm from its plates, fractures, smoke and chips, and from how a blow on it sounds. Breaking the arm feels earned, and choosing between breaking it and saving it for the bait is a real choice.
6. The Molten Fist is fair and terrifying: the cone shows exactly where it will split, the lock says when to commit, being far away doesn't save you, getting out sideways close in or behind it does, and the chasm feels like the biggest thing in the game.
7. The blue flash before the guard reads in time to hold or release a counter.
8. The charge, the leap, the stomps' cracks and vents and the swing each read in time, at camera distance and at night; leaps and swings come only from far off, and the swing's wide circle looks like it has weight behind it.
9. The entrance out of the ground lands, and the fight sounds as heavy as it looks.
10. 1,500 health with its recklessness makes a fight of a few minutes, not a slog.

### Feel test (the rival)

Play the Rival Fight a few times, then the Melee Duel. This is the part expected to take the most tuning; notes on any of these help:

1. Its stance reads with no label: in phase 1 it keeps its distance (zipping between vantages, cutting and boosting away when you crowd it) and never rushes you; in phase 2 it comes for you and only calls strikes or dives when you run.
2. The fight needs no words: the shield, its cracks, the stun, the guard, the riposte and the red flash all read from the model, the effects and the sound. The red flash's charge and sound are unmistakable at any distance.
3. Its dash chains feel like a mech manoeuvring, not a homing missile: the side legs make you track it, and the cut's glint arrives early enough to parry or dash.
4. Phase 1's answers work in 3D: slashing bolts back into the shield, tethering and kicking its grenade, getting behind cover from the beam once its charge-up starts.
5. Phase 2's tells read at camera distance and in the dark: the guard, the gold and red flashes, the dive's charge, leap and flash, the strike rings and their sirens.
6. The red flash is fair on every difficulty: it commits to its line the moment it flares, so a dash through it reaches its back in time, while walking round from in front can't.
7. At range in phase 2 it mixes long dash chains with the odd dive bomb rather than leaping at you over and over.
8. The cutscenes frame it well and don't overstay; the camera comes back facing it.

### Feel test (AEGIS-07 as the player)

Pick AEGIS-07 on the title screen and play the Wave Demo, then Endless to about wave 10 and the Rival Fight. The numbers are first guesses at keeping it level with RONIN; notes on any of these help:

1. Gunner mode zones: the machine gun's damage is steady but doesn't stop a scrapper reaching you, so you keep moving and spend the beam (and your shield) on lines of enemies or a brute.
2. Melee mode gets enemies off you: the swipes and thrust feel heavy and forceful, the block reads as the blade raised, and the drawn-back burst feels worth holding.

### Feel test (camera and slashes)

1. Scrolling in and out feels smooth, and first person works for running, slashing and the tether.
2. A wall cling holds as long as you hold a movement key and drops the moment you let go.

### Feel test (movement and the site)

1. A reversal at a sprint reads as a skid on the hand (sparks, marks) and gets you going the other way no slower than before.
2. Vaults read as a hop to the side over the car, not a step up onto it.
3. A wall cling gives a moment to look round before the slide, and a kick from it goes where you expect.
4. Slashing at a drone overhead or at something below a ledge lands without fighting the camera, and level play still swings level.
5. Holding attack in the air to plunge doesn't fire by accident when you meant a tap.

### Feel test (Phase 4)

Play Endless in the crossroads to about wave 10, with a Blackout wave if one comes (Endless picks twists at random from wave 4). The Phase 3 checks still apply. Then check:

1. The camera keeps you readable everywhere: the plaza, the alleys, under the overpass, on the fire escapes and roofs.
2. Fights spread across levels: enemies climb after you, and the roofs and girders are worth using rather than a place to hide.
3. Every tell reads in the dark corners and in a Blackout, and the flicker never hides one.
4. Rain and ash add atmosphere without noise, and Retro looks like the 2D game's pixels.

### Feel test (Phase 3)

Play the Wave Demo, then Endless to about wave 10, with no text on screen. The Phase 1 and 2 checks still apply. Then check:

1. Each enemy reads at camera distance: you can tell what it's about to do from its model, glint, laser or arc, and its sound when it's behind you.
2. The answers in the table above work without fighting the camera, in particular getting behind a shield scrapper and timing a drone parry.
3. Enemies that follow you up a fire escape or onto a roof look deliberate, not stuck, and standing somewhere they can't reach is a breather rather than an exploit (gunners, grenadiers, drones and snipers still reach you).
4. Upgrade picks and wave twists read without the 2D text, and a heavy wave (brutes) feels fair.

## Keeping up with the 2D game

The 3D lane forks the 2D files it can reuse instead of loading them, so changes on either side never break the other. `SYNCED_FROM` holds the 2D commit the copies last caught up with.

1. `node tools/sync-2d.js` adds the 2D repo as the `2d` remote, fetches it, and lists the 2D commits and files changed since `SYNCED_FROM`. Each file points at its 3D counterpart: names match where the role matches (`js/rival.js` ports `js/rival.js`), and the 2D `main.js` maps to `core.js` plus `main.js`.
2. Port what you want by hand (`--diff` prints the full diff).
3. `node tools/sync-2d.js --mark` records the 2D commit you caught up to.

Saves are separate: `slashyslash3d.settings.v1` and `slashyslash3d.save.v1`. Both games can run from the same origin (`file://` or the same local server), so shared keys would let rebinding keys here rebind the 2D game too.

**Lighting.** The scene has no ambient light. When an arena loads, light from the street lamps, neon signs and barrel fires is baked into the vertices of every surface (cut into cells of at most 1 m, 2 m on the tall boundary walls, and only where a cell isn't buried in another box) with shadow rays against the boxes. It's baked in three channels: steady lamps, fires and buzzing lights (neon and two failing lamps). Each frame the shader scales the fire channel by a waver and the buzz channel by an occasional stutter, so flicker shows on the world without rebaking. Characters take the same split light where they stand, as Half-Life 1 did, and everything also receives the dynamic pool. Loading an arena (collision, nav graph, bake and meshes) takes about 300 ms; the Blackout rebakes only the steady channel, in about 40 ms. A lamp going out rebakes just the vertices in its reach, 1,200 a frame.

Two Three.js rules from the plan apply: the light count never changes (a fixed pool of 8 for effects, unused ones at zero intensity), so shaders never recompile when effects come and go, and every material is compiled while the page loads (the smoke test fails if a shader compiles mid-fight); and each character is one draw call, so a fight with seven scrappers is about 30 draw calls before gibs.

## Differences from the plan

- **Two repos instead of a `3d/` directory.** Paths the plan writes as `3d/...` are at this repo's root. The sync diff runs through the `2d` remote instead of within one repo.
- **Movement is retuned for 3D**, as the plan said it would be after the feel test, toward Warframe's flow more than the 2D game's measured pace. Ground speed builds from a run to a sprint; turns carve the velocity round (7.5 rad/s at a sprint, 22 when slow) instead of accelerating straight at the new direction; reversals skid; stops take 0.2 s instead of 0.09. The 1 m step-up became 0.45 m, and anything up to 1.95 m is vaulted at speed, which replaces the instant pop onto car bodies. Gravity went from 87 to 70 m/s² with less pull near the top of a held jump; jump speeds were rescaled so every height is unchanged, so every climb in the level still works. The slide starts closer to your speed (x1.2 rather than a jump to 23 m/s) and lasts longer. Combat launches were left alone and hang a little longer.
- **The sprint pose** pitches the torso up to about 45 degrees, trails the blade behind with the arm straight and pumps the free arm; the whole body banks into turns from the sideways acceleration. Movement poses crossfade over 0.12 s; combat poses keep the 2D timing exactly.
- **Crouch defaults to `C` only, not `C`/`Ctrl`.** Holding Ctrl to crouch while pressing W to move forward is Ctrl+W, which closes the browser tab, and pages can't block it. Ctrl can still be bound in the second slot.
- **The player model** follows the concept sheets loosely: charcoal armour with a voxel noise texture, a red right arm, a glowing visor slit, silver fins on the helmet, gold fittings, small red running lights, a red scarf fraying to black, and a katana whose curved blade glows. It is boxes and hexagonal prisms on bones, like the scrapper, so it stays one draw call and breaks into gibs.
- **Poses come from the 2D game.** `PlayerPose` runs the 2D pose function's logic (feet and hand targets on the side plane, lean, flips) and maps the targets into 3D with two-bone IK. Swings point the blade along the same 3D arc the trail is drawn on. After 2.5 s at rest the blade goes back in its sheath and the left hand rests on the hilt, as on the concept sheet.
- **Characters get a cool rim light** (not in the plan). The dark armour disappeared against the night street without it.
- **Pulled forward from Phase 3: the gunner and the grenadier**, because Phase 2's bolt returns and grenade kicks need something to answer. Each has its 2D tell in 3D: the gunner's laser (dashed while it tracks, solid white once locked, ending on you) and the grenadier's arc with its blast zone drawn on the ground, which stays under the grenade while it flies.
- **Bullet time** desaturates lit surfaces in their shader instead of a separate screen pass; glowing parts and every effect stay bright, which is what the 2D emissive layer did.
- **Ground marks start hot.** Gouges, cracks and scorches glow orange and cool to pale scars lit by the street; dark marks on the dark asphalt were invisible.
- **Barrels sit on the 2D barrel spots**: five on the street, one on the overpass, one on a roof.
- **Pulled forward from Phase 3:** the camera rule for attack slots (an attacker more than 75 degrees off the view winds up 1.3x longer), glints drawn over walls at a minimum size, threat ring markers for wind-ups, glints and the direction a hit came from, pickups and damage numbers. Feel-test item 2 needs them.
- **The street's sides.** The 2D buildings become solid blocks behind the street with walkable roofs, and a row of facades too tall to climb closes the front, so the street is a 14 m canyon. Platforms are solid slabs you mantle onto rather than one-way ledges.
- **Navigation is one graph, not a grid per layer.** Every box top where a body fits gets a node every 0.5 m. Nodes link to neighbours within a 1 m step; surface edges link to surfaces up to 6.4 m above (a jump, stepping out from under an overhang first when a platform is stacked overhead, as on the fire escapes) and to whatever lies below (a drop). The flow field is a Dijkstra search back from the player, re-solved at most ten times a second and only within about 60 m of travel. Nodes on lower surfaces near the player are seeded at a cost, so enemies that can't climb to you wait below rather than wandering. The graph takes about 100 ms to build at load and a re-solve about 2 ms.
- **Enemy jumps are stronger than in 2D.** A 2D scrapper's jump tops out just short of the first tier of platforms, so in 2D melee enemies stay on the street. Here they jump up to 6.4 m, so a roof isn't a safe spot from melee unless it's high. Brutes are the exception and stay on the ground.
- **The drone's glint is timed to its impact.** In 2D the drone glints before its dive starts, and the dive's flight then puts the hit outside a parry made on the glint. Here the glint comes 0.28 s before the dive reaches you, as every other glint does.
- **Sniper perches are at ledge edges**, the middle of each side of a qualifying ledge, since a sniper in the middle of a 3 m balcony can't see past its own floor. A sniper rises off its ledge, zips along a straight line its whole body clears, and drops onto the new perch.
- **Glints Only** hides the threat ring's position and wind-up markers (the 3D ring stands in for the 2D `!` markers), keeping the glints and hit directions.
- **Spawn beams.** An arriving enemy shows as a narrowing column of light for 0.75 s, as the 2D spawn beam did.
- **The boundary walls continue upward as invisible collision**, so a zip from the highest roofs can't land on top of one and leave the arena.
- **The crossroads follows the plan's layout** with a few specifics: streets 18 m wide (the rule is 12), alleys exactly 6 m, the corner blocks set back so the plaza is 20 m square and raised 15 cm. Fire escapes have tiers 5.4 m apart from 5.5 m up, so there are 5 m between them and an enemy's 6.4 m jump reaches each. Container barricades (cars and containers are the only things at ground level you can get behind, keeping the plan's no-cover rule) close the street ends; the boundary is a ring of 26 m facades with invisible collision above.
- **The 2D street stays** as a free roam and as the fixture the movement checks were written against.
- **Retro is a toggle** in Visuals rather than a preset that changes other settings.
- **No glow pass.** Glowing parts are fullbright and effects additive, which covers what the 2D glow did; a bloom pass would mean vendoring Three's post-processing.
- **Not in the plan: the construction site and crane.** One corner block of the crossroads became a construction site with a tower crane whose jib and hanging load are tether points; the tether reaches them from 42 m (21 m otherwise) and snaps to them a little more readily. A tap flings you on an arc that tops out over the point and drops you onto it; a hold hauls you out round the beam's side and onto its top.
- **Not in the plan: skid turns, side vaults, wall clings, aimed swings, hold-to-plunge and breakable lamps**, asked for after Phase 4. A plunge is now a held attack in the air rather than `C` plus attack, so there's no air heavy; air strings are lights.
- **AEGIS-07 is a mech, not the player's rig.** The plan had the rival on the player's controller and rig, as in 2D. Its reference sheets show a 2.5 m mech with a thruster pack, a shoulder cannon and a forearm blade, so it has its own model and poses, and it moves by running, thruster dashes, hovering and leaps instead of the player's double jump, wall jump and tether zip. Its visor beam became the shoulder cannon's beam.
- **Not in the plan: stances and dash chains.** Zoning against charging, and dashes taken as chains of legs relative to the player (side to side, round the flank, a feint, then the cut), were asked for with the rival.
- **The plan's 3D rules for the rival's line-bound moves are in:** the red flash faces you as it flares and holds that line to the end (it tracked you until its eye flashed, changed on feedback), then everything but a 90 degree wedge behind it is hit (out to 20 m, and its body blocks walking through); the cleave and the dive landing throw rings. The plan's phase 2 eruptions were built and then taken out on feedback; they came back in a controlled form as the orbital strikes it calls when you run.
